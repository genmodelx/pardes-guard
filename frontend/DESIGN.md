# Pardes Guard design system

This file is the plan the screens are built from. If a screen needs something that is not here, add it
here and to `/styleguide` first, then use it.

## 1. Who we design for

A person leaving home for the first time, for work or study. They hold a low-cost Android phone, often on
a weak connection. Many read Urdu or another South Asian language, some read very little. They have never
dealt with a visa, and an agent is telling them to hurry and pay.

So every screen has to answer three questions without reading much: where am I, what do I do now, and can
I trust this.

## 2. Concept: a passport that protects you

The look comes from travel papers: passport pages, entry stamps, boarding passes and the fine line
patterns printed on passports and banknotes.

- These are inspiration only. No national emblems, seals or flags. Nothing is laid out like a real
  government form, and the product name is on every screen, so it cannot be taken for an official site.
- **One loud thing: the ink stamp.** It lands when a check finishes, and it marks finished journey stages.
  Everything else is calm. No other entrance animation exists in the app.
- Countries are named in the reader's language. A flag appears in one place only: as the picture on a
  country choice in the start questions, so a person who reads little can find their country. Flags are
  never decoration and never a sign of authority.

- Red, amber and OK green are status colours. They are never decoration.

- The dark theme follows the phone setting and can be forced in Settings (`data-theme` on `<html>`).

No soft drop shadows. Depth comes from the paper/sheet contrast and 1px rules. The stamp and the passport
pages are the only things allowed to rotate or overlap an edge.

### Security-print pattern

`public/patterns/guilloche.svg` is a band of fine interlaced lines, generated by
`scripts/build-pattern.mjs`. It is drawn at low contrast in `--rule`.

It appears in exactly three places: beside the brand on the language picker, as a strip under the top bar
on Home, and along the top edge of each passport page. It is never placed behind body text.

## 4. Type

| Script | Family | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Latin | Atkinson Hyperlegible Next | English, Filipino, Indonesian, all digits |
| Urdu, Punjabi (Shahmukhi) | Noto Nastaliq Urdu | `ur`, `pa` |
| Pashto, Arabic, Sindhi | Noto Naskh Arabic | `ps`, `ar`, `sd` |
| Hindi, Nepali | Noto Sans Devanagari | `hi`, `ne` |
| Bengali | Noto Sans Bengali | `bn` |
| Sinhala | Noto Sans Sinhala | `si` |
| Tamil | Noto Sans Tamil | `ta` |
| Codes | Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono | share code, case reference, licence number, a file's fingerprint |

- Fonts are the Google Fonts files, served from our own `/fonts` folder. Each `@font-face` has a
  `unicode-range`, so a phone only downloads the script that is on the page. The active locale's font is
  preloaded. The language picker writes other languages in the phone's own fonts so it downloads nothing.
- Digits are always Western digits (0–9) in every language. Amounts always carry their currency.
- Atkinson's zero is slashed, which reads as a letter on an amount. "Pardes Zero", a one-glyph font with
  a plain zero, comes first in the text stacks. The mono face keeps the slashed zero for codes.

Scale (mobile base 18px):

| Token | Size | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--t-display` | 36px | 800 | one per screen at most: welcome, verdict line |
| `--t-h1` | 28px | 800 | screen title |
| `--t-h2` | 22px | 700 | section title, next-step title |
| `--t-body` | 18px | 400 | everything people read |
| `--t-small` | 15px | 400 | sources, dates, helper text. Never smaller. |

- Line height 1.5 for body and 1.2 for titles. Nastaliq uses 2.0 for body and 1.8 for titles, at 1.1× size,
  because its letters stack downwards. Naskh and the Indic scripts use 1.7.
- Sentence case everywhere. Capitals only inside the stamp.
- Left-aligned (start-aligned) text. Nothing is centred except the stamp word and the bottom bar labels.
- Lines stay under about 65 characters.

## 5. Layout and navigation

- Designed at 360 × 800 first. One column, `--col` wide at most, centred on larger screens.
- **Top bar:** Back (icon and word) at the start, SOS at the end, on every screen. Home shows the brand
  instead of Back. The screen title is an `h1` in the page, not in the bar, so long Urdu titles can wrap.
- **Bottom bar:** Journey, Check, Help, More. Icon above label. The active item has a filled icon, bold
  label and a bar above it. On screens 900px and wider it becomes a side rail.
- **One primary action per screen**, full width, in the bottom third. Secondary actions are outlined.
- Every choice is an icon plus a label. Icons come from one set (Phosphor). No emoji.
- Right-to-left: layout uses CSS logical properties only (`tests/design.test.ts` fails the build on
  `left`/`right` properties). Icons that show direction (back, next, plane, progress) flip; others do not.
- Phone numbers, amounts, codes and links are wrapped in `<bdi dir="ltr">` so they never reorder inside
  Urdu text.

## 6. The stamp

The signature of the product. Component: `components/ui/Stamp.tsx`.

| Level | Word (en / ur) | Frame shape | Icon | Ink | Tilt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| red | STOP / رکیں | octagon | raised hand | `--red` | −6° |
| amber | CHECK / جانچیں | rectangle with scooped corners | warning triangle | `--amber` | −3° |
| green | LOOKS OK / ٹھیک لگتا ہے | pill | tick | `--ok` | 4° |
| done (journey stage) | DONE / مکمل | round date stamp | tick | `--green` | −8° to 8°, fixed per stage |

- Three independent signals per verdict: frame shape, icon and word. Colour is the fourth.
- Green says "looks OK", never "safe" or "genuine". The app cannot prove a document is real.
- The icon sits above the word, both centred in the frame. The word steps down in size as it gets
  longer, so "LOOKS OK" fits where "STOP" does.
- A second small line carries the plain meaning ("Do not pay yet") or, for stages, the date. In
  Arabic-script languages the verdict stamps drop this line: Nastaliq is not legible at that size, and
  the same sentence is the heading right under the stamp.
- **Ink.** Three SVG filters, `#pg-ink-1` to `#pg-ink-3`, roughen the edges (turbulence + displacement)
  and knock small specks out of the ink; a stamp picks one from its seed, so two stamps on a page do not
  share a texture. In the light theme the stamp multiplies onto the card so print shows through.
- **Press.** When a result first appears the stamp comes down in 300ms: it starts larger and tilted a
  little further, hits at 70%, settles. The phone gives one short vibration if it can. Then it stays.
  It does not replay when the user comes back to the page. A stage stamp lands the first time its
  passport page is seen complete.
- **Reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the stamp is simply there. No vibration.
- Screen readers get `role="img"` with the full sentence, and the same verdict is the page's `h1`.

## 8. Words

- Short sentences, everyday words, about a 10-year-old's reading level.
- Buttons say what happens: "Check this paper", not "Submit".
- A term is explained the first time it appears.
- Errors never blame and never stop at "something went wrong". They say what to do next.
- Draft content is always labelled draft. Sample data is always labelled sample.
- A language drafted by machine says so on every screen, in that language, and offers the English page.
- Practice is labelled practice. AI feedback is never called a score, a pass or a prediction.
- The app never says a document is genuine.

## 9. Motion

- The stamp press (section 6) is the only choreographed motion.
- Controls may change colour in 120ms on press. Dialogs appear without animation.
- While a tapped screen is still downloading, a thin green bar grows across the top of the window. It
  waits 150ms before showing, so a fast tap shows nothing.
- No fade-in or slide-up on page content. Content that is loading shows a skeleton of the same shape.
- While a voice conversation is listening, its waveform icon breathes, like the running check step. Reduced
  motion stops both.

the person's own), and so does the line under the title, which links back to it.

### Staff screens (NGO console, admin)

Staff work at a desk as often as on a phone, so these two screens may grow to `--col-wide`. Each block
is a plain sheet: a report with its note and two buttons, a ring with its linked agencies, an alert
with its severity chip, a warning with its reasons and Publish. The admin import shows its preview
(counts, skipped rows, the first five firms) before the one button that saves. A guest trying them
sees a quiet info notice at the top on every staff screen.

## 11. Quality floor

- WCAG 2.2 AA. Contrast 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for control borders and focus. Focus is always visible:
  a 3px `--focus` outline with a 2px gap.
- Works at 200% text size and with the keyboard alone. Skip link on every page.
- Simple mode raises every size by 15% (`--user-scale`) and hides the rows wrapped in `Advanced`.
- Tap targets are at least 52px.
- Every page is plain HTML in the right language and direction before any script runs.
- Screens are checked at 360px and 1280px, in English and Urdu, light and dark, and with axe-core. Hindi,
  Bengali, Nepali and Filipino are checked on the busiest screens (home, journey, a red result, SOS, a
  case letter, eligibility, interview, More) in the light theme.
